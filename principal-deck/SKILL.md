# Principal Deck

Produce a presentation that a principal-level person can **defend under hostile
questioning**, not just present. The deliverable is four artifacts:

1. `deck.md` — the frozen content (arguments, structure, every claim).
2. `deck.html` — the deck itself, built from `assets/template.html`.
3. `deck.pdf` — the export/fallback, generated by `scripts/make-pdf.mjs`.
4. `q-and-a.md` — a hostile Q&A pack (≈20 questions with 60–90s answers).

Read `references/content-doctrine.md` before writing content,
`references/deck-mechanics.md` before touching the HTML, and
`references/qa-pack.md` before writing the Q&A pack.

## Phase 0 — Interrogate the brief

Before writing anything, establish (ask the user if the brief doesn't say):

- **Audience & personas**: who is in the room, and what does each one attack?
  (CFO attacks money, CDO attacks data realism, COO attacks accountability,
  CMO attacks adoption, architects attack depth.)
- **The decision sought**: what should the room approve when you stop talking?
  A deck without an ask is a lecture.
- **Time budget**: total minutes for presentation and for Q&A. The time budget
  drives slide count, fragment count, and the pace clock targets.
- **Hostility level**: friendly readout vs. panel that probes three questions
  deep. Default to hostile — it only improves the deck.
- **What the presenter can truthfully own**: never put a claim, number, or
  war story on a slide the presenter cannot defend three follow-ups deep.

## Phase 1 — Content before pixels

Write `deck.md` first: every slide's argument in prose, plus the appendix
cards. Get it approved, then treat it as **frozen** — after the freeze,
wording and visuals may improve but arguments and structure change only as a
documented exception. This is what makes the deck rehearsable.

Use the narrative spine (adapt, don't worship — but every element must earn
its slide):

1. **Recommendation up front** — the answer in the first minute, then "the
   next N minutes are how".
2. **The goal in their words** — what the audience's organization wants,
   unpacked into its real demands.
3. **Your reading of the problem** — a compression, not a recital of their
   brief. State assumptions as assumptions; corrections are gifts.
4. **Positions taken** — the 2–4 decisions that determine whether this works,
   each with the rejected alternative named. Stated, not implied.
5. **The solution / architecture** — built in the order the data (or value)
   flows, ending in named capabilities, not product logos.
6. **First use cases** — concrete, sequenced, each tied to who it serves.
7. **Value** — the slide the whole thing stands on. Grammar:
   **baseline → projected → tracked**, one owner and one review forum per KPI.
8. **Roadmap + risks** — phases as what each phase *earns*, waves with gates,
   and the top 3–4 risks each paired with its mitigation (cards, not a
   footnote — risks as an afterthought read as risks unmanaged).
9. **Operating model** — who runs this on a Monday morning: owners, funding,
   decision rights, governance.
10. **The ask + echo** — one picture of the whole argument, the 3–4 takeaways
    the room must remember an hour later, then the explicit ask. Then stop.

**Appendix = pocket cards**: one card per predicted attack (identity, build
vs. buy, compliance, channel conflict, deep technical trade-offs…). Never
presented; jumped to mid-Q&A.

## Phase 2 — The bar every component passes

Test each slide, each element, each sentence against:

- **Altitude**: portfolio-level, multi-year; detail lives in the appendix.
- **Money**: every value claim has a baseline, a projected movement, a
  tracking mechanism, an owner, and a forum where it is read. "We'll measure
  it" without the noun (which KPI, what baseline, who tracks it) fails.
- **Ownership**: every decision, KPI, and risk has a named role attached.
- **Brevity**: each beat speakable in 60–90 seconds, answer first.
- **Strong case**: every component on the slide makes a case. Cut
  self-referential copy ("this slide shows…"), designer legends the visual
  already explains, and any line the presenter wouldn't say out loud.
- **Honesty**: no invented statistics; external numbers only from named,
  checkable sources (state who commissioned them); personal claims only at
  the strength the presenter can defend ("seen first-hand" ≠ "delivered").

When reviewing the user's content, **flag, don't remove** — the presenter
decides what gets cut. Refuse vague answers during content work: demand the
noun.

## Phase 3 — Build the deck

Copy `assets/template.html` and follow `references/deck-mechanics.md`. The
non-negotiables:

- **Single file**, no external dependencies; works from `file://`.
- **Fragments are keypress beats**: one keypress = one spoken beat. Count
  total keypresses and keep the number in the title-slide notes.
- **Speaker notes live in `data-notes`** on each slide, in the notes grammar
  (`TT m:ss` budget, `||` beat separators, quoted text = words actually
  spoken, ALL-CAPS labels for pocket ammo and untanglers).
- **Presenter chrome**: pace clock with cumulative per-slide targets, slide
  jump map, appendix quick-jump, cycling notes panel. All hidden in print.
- **PDF export** via `scripts/make-pdf.mjs`; verify the layout by reading the
  rendered PDF pages (as images), not by trusting the CSS.

## Phase 4 — The Q&A pack and rehearsal kit

Follow `references/qa-pack.md`. Deliver ~20 persona-attributed hostile
questions with answers in the delivery format (answer first, one supporting
structure, 60–90 seconds, stop), a kill-list of phrases the presenter must
never say, and a drill list of fluency gaps (cost-of-inaction inversion,
rapid recall of KPIs with baselines, per-slide 30-second cold explains).

## Working style

- One question at a time when interrogating the user; short, bold-led replies.
- Commit progress often if in a git repo.
- The presenter originates positions; you red-team, stress-test, assemble,
  and polish. Never let the deck say something the presenter didn't decide.
