# Audience prep: the Q&A pack

The Q&A decides the room more than the delivery does. Build `q-and-a.md` as
a rehearsal script, not a FAQ.

**Scale to the scenario** (from the Phase-0 hostility read):

- **Hostile / high-stakes** — everything below: ~20 persona questions,
  kill-list, drill list, timed mocks.
- **Standard** — the 8–10 likeliest questions with answers in the same
  format, plus one timed run.
- **Friendly** — fold anticipated questions into the speaker notes as
  `POCKET AMMO` beats; keep the answer format, skip the pack.

The answer format and the honesty rails apply at every level; only the
volume of preparation scales.

## Question generation

Write ~20 questions, each attributed to a persona, ordered roughly as the
attack would unfold. Generate them by walking the personas over the deck:

- **CFO**: "What do I get in 90 days and how do I know it wasn't happening
  anyway?" · "Why fund this now — what does waiting cost me?" · "When do I
  see revenue?" · "Budget halves in month six — what do you kill?"
- **CDO / data leadership**: realism attacks. "X before Y — how?" · "When the
  data model changes, your baselines shift. What then?"
- **CIO / architects**: "Why this platform and not best-of-breed?" · "How
  deep are you hands-on, really?" (the honest-depth answer: name exactly
  what you've built, map the concepts, and say where you'd bring
  specialists — asking beats bluffing).
- **COO**: accountability ambush. "The automation ships a bad thing to two
  million customers — who is accountable and what happens in the next hour?"
- **CMO / business**: adoption, prioritization ("month nine, a region
  proposes something not in your plan — what happens?"), measurement forums.
- **Channel / sales**: conflict-of-interest attacks; the commitments you
  will never break with partner data.
- **The smiling one**: "Did you use AI to prepare this?" — answer with the
  operating model you'd apply to their business: humans originate and stay
  accountable, AI red-teams and accelerates inside guardrails.

## Answer format (every answer)

1. **Answer first** — the conclusion in the first sentence.
2. **One supporting structure** — a single mechanism, example, or number.
3. **60–90 seconds, then stop.** Silence after a good answer is strength.
   Catching yourself starting a third example means you're already too long.

Write answers as spoken prose, first person, in the presenter's own decided
positions. Numbers appear with their baseline and their source's sponsor
("commissioned by the vendor" said out loud buys credibility).

## Kill-list

Maintain an explicit list of things the presenter must never say: debunked
statistics, guarantees ("I can guarantee"), retracted positions, client
names in failure stories, claims above defensible strength. Check every
answer against it.

## Drill list (fluency, not content)

The gaps that show up under pressure, worth timed drilling:

- **Lede burial**: assembling answers that lead with plumbing instead of the
  business get. Drill answer-first assembly on cold questions.
- **Dropped ingredients**: elements fine in isolation (e.g. attribution)
  that vanish from assembled answers.
- **The inversion**: "waiting a year means…" said aloud until it's reflex.
- **Rapid recall**: N use cases with sponsor/KPI/baseline each in 90 seconds;
  per-slide 30-second cold explains.
- **The echo**: name the 3–4 takeaways the room must remember an hour later;
  they become the closing slide and the cut-test for every rehearsal minute.

## Rehearsal protocol

- Full timed runs against the pace clock; the #1 mock metric is total time.
  **If the run is long: cut speech, not slides.**
- One rehearsal in the real setup (screen share, camera, visible timer).
- Grade answers on: lede first? one structure? stopped in time? nothing from
  the kill-list?
